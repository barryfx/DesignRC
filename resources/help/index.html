What DesignRC does

DesignRC is a desktop CAD application for designing built-up radio-control airplane wings. You describe one half-wing with one or more connected panels. DesignRC interpolates the airfoil and planform, generates the internal structure and manufacturing geometry, then mirrors the result into a complete wing.

  • Imports Selig-style root and tip airfoil .dat files.
  • Interpolates airfoil shape, chord, sweep, twist, and rib stations across each panel.
  • Builds solid ribs, riblets, spars, shear webs, sheeting, leading and trailing edges, control surfaces, hinge posts, spoilers, wiring holes, and fixed or removable joiners.
  • Displays a material-colored Open CASCADE solid assembly in an interactive 3D viewport.
  • Creates a flattened, annotated, full-scale technical plan of both wing halves.
  • Exports plans to PDF/DXF/SVG, laser-cut parts to DXF/SVG/PDF, and the 3D assembly to STEP.
  • Saves editable projects as .designrc files.
Design convention: panel parameters describe the right half-wing from the aircraft centerline outward. DesignRC generates that half and mirrors it for the complete preview, plan, and assembly.

New in 1.8.0 — November 5, 2026

  • Sheet Entire Panel: a one-time button enables all four sheeting options and sets their stop ribs to the panel's last rib.
  • Parallel to: align a straight spar with geometry in its current panel. Curved references use their end-to-end direction, Tip Chord Location becomes read-only, and Top/Bottom/Mid placement is retained. Saved references update with their geometry and are checked for missing or circular links.
  • Spar/joiner alignment: Generate Wing shows one Continue/Cancel warning when matching root chord locations have different directions. New-install defaults link both wood spars parallel to the joiner.
  • Collision recovery: rear-spar relocation works with linked spars, switching the moved spar to Manual. Rejected moves restore its original reference and locations.
  • Spoiler placement: manual chord placement allows spoilers between front and rear spars. Immediately Behind Spar sits below Start Chord Location and disables it when checked. A dropdown beside the checkbox selects among multiple top spars; the selection is saved. Clearance accounts for tilted ribs and rail ends at rib faces.

  • Control direction: Perpendicular to Ribs makes the aileron or flap hinge perpendicular to the rib axis; Root Width sets its chordwise width at the first control rib.
  • Control construction: top and bottom skins extend from the hinge post or spar to the TE without a Width field. New-project hinge spars default to 3/8 inch wide, 1/4 inch high, and a 35-degree hinge angle.
  • Sleeves: separate OD and ID fields define each sleeve. ID starts 1 mm below OD, both panel defaults match, and mirrored root sleeves retain their bore and outside diameter.

  • Generation progress: ailerons and flaps now report rib, stock, skin, collision-check, and mesh stages. Panel completion appears after the controls finish.

Earlier: 1.7.0 — November 3, 2026

  • Built-up controls: ailerons and flaps can have separate ribs, top/bottom skins, and additional Top/Bottom wood or carbon spars. A divider separates the control groups. Every new parameter is saved in projects and available in Edit > Defaults.
  • Hinge choices: matching wing/control Hinge Posts or four Top/Bottom Hinge Spars. Spar mode adds Width, Height, and Hinge Angle, measured aft from vertical. Ribs and moving spars share the bevel, and all four spars finish at the moving end-rib faces. Only the selected construction's fields appear.

Skins automatically extend from the aft face of their hinge post or spar to the TE, with no skin Width parameter. Manual control-skin taper options are removed. Sheets stop at both sides of hinge and spanwise gaps.
  • Part names: ARn and FRn identify aileron/flap ribs everywhere; ATE1, ATE2, etc. and FTE1, FTE2, etc. identify their stock TE parts. Plans include spar, skin-thickness, and TE-stock dimension leaders. Shared rib labels no longer overlap, false full-chord boundary lines are removed, and hinge-side rib contours no longer double back at the top spar.
  • Joiner length: Number of Rib Bays applies to fixed wood/tube/rod joiners and removable sleeves/rods. One bay crosses two ribs, including R1a when present. The selected span must fit both adjoining panels. Alignment pins keep their existing lengths. Joiners that leave rib boundaries are rejected with the joiner and rib identified.
  • Stock and spar fixes: LE/post height equality is accepted, undersized hinge posts report their minimum height, curved/twisted control spars are fitted, exported ribs retain TE-sheet recesses, and deleting all spars no longer restores obsolete legacy spars on reload.
  • Plan exports: Export Plan now offers PDF, DXF, and SVG before the filename dialog. All preserve the complete 1:1 layout. DXF retains RGB line and label colors with Autodesk-compatible document records; SVG also retains fill colors and transparency. Labels are outlined in DXF/SVG to avoid missing fonts.

Earlier: 1.6.0 — October 5, 2026

Panels can now use dimensioned SVG/DXF leading- and trailing-edge curves. Smooth guide sweeps follow the imported shape, airfoil changes, and twist, including the upright center-root transition. Straight hinges remain available for curved control surfaces, and adjacent panel chords are matched within the 1/4 inch (6.35 mm) tolerance.

The filename and curve geometry are saved inside the project and restored on load. See Imported LE/TE curves for file requirements, axis handling, tolerances, joint matching, and stock behavior.

Recommended workflow

  1. Choose the number of connected half-wing panels.
  2. Set each panel's span, chords, sweep, dihedral, twist, airfoils, and ribs.
  3. Add spars, sheeting, edge stock, joiners, controls, spoilers, wiring holes, and optional lightening features.
  4. Press Generate Wing to validate the design and build the complete 3D assembly.
  5. Inspect the assembly with orbit, pan, zoom, Fit View, and the preset camera buttons.
  6. Press Generate Plan to build the full-scale flattened drawing.
  7. Save the project and export the plan, selected parts, or STEP assembly.

Editing parameters marks the existing preview and plan as out of date. DesignRC does not automatically rebuild expensive geometry, allowing several related values to be changed first.

Units and value entry

The project-wide length unit is millimeters or inches. Geometry is stored internally in millimeters, so changing display units does not resize the wing.

Global, mm, and in selectors

The User Defaults dialog shows a selector beside every length:

Global
Use the current global length unit.
mm
Always display and edit this parameter in millimeters.
in
Always display and edit this parameter in inches.

Per-parameter unit overrides are saved and are also used for matching annotations.

Entering measurements

  • Inch controls accept decimals, fractions such as 3/16, and mixed values such as 1 1/2.
  • Spinner arrows advance inch fields by 1/32 inch. Exact 1/32-inch values display as reduced fractions.
  • You may type an explicit suffix such as 12 mm, 0.5 in, or 1/4". The field converts the value and adopts that explicit unit override.
  • Angle fields are always displayed in degrees. Percent fields represent chordwise position, accept manually entered values with up to two decimal places, and retain one-percent arrow increments.

Multi-panel wings

Number of Wing Panels
Selects 1–12 connected panels in one half-wing. Changing the count adds or removes panel editors after confirmation where necessary.
Panel 1
The center panel. Its root chord and root airfoil are editable, and it can use Rib 1a and centerline-only features.
Panels 2–12
Each outer panel inherits the preceding panel's tip chord and tip airfoil as its root values.

Shared ribs are oriented to bisect the angle between adjoining panels. The 3D model includes dihedral, while the plan flattens the panels to true manufacturing length.

Dimensions

Flattened root-to-tip length of this half-wing panel.

Airfoil chord at the panel ends. Without imported curves, Root Chord is editable only on Panel 1; outer panels inherit the adjoining tip chord. Imported curves supply read-only root and tip chords, with the joint-matching rules described under Imported LE/TE curves.

Rib Count

Add Bottom Rear Build Tabs / Add Bottom Front Build Tabs / Add Top Rear Build Tabs / Add Top Front Build Tabs

Below the divider, independently enable any of the four temporary construction supports. Top tabs support upside-down building. Each checkbox reveals its own inset Location field: front defaults to 15% chord and rear to 75%. The options work with or without Tip Twist and do not change the wing's twist.

Tab Width is shared by all tabs and defaults to 3/16 inch (4.7625 mm). Height Above Work Table defaults to zero and extends all enabled tabs by the specified clearance from their support plane. Both fields follow global units and accept explicit inch or millimeter entry; Tab Width must be positive and height cannot be negative.

Tabs move to clear spar notches on their side; top tabs also avoid spoilers. By default, sheeting on the same side omits the affected tab on that rib. Add Tabs to Ribs With Sheeting keeps those tabs for construction before sheeting is installed. The tabs pass through intact sheeting solids in the preview and STEP assembly; no slots are cut. Remove the temporary tabs before finishing the wing. Unresolvable spar collisions still report an error.

Add Tab/Rib Separation Line starts unchecked. When checked, 2D rib exports include an open line along the original airfoil contour across each actual tab for sanding or cutting. The line stops 0.25 mm short of each tab side (one-quarter of the width for tabs narrower than 1 mm). It uses a separate TAB_RIB_SEPARATION DXF layer and blue SVG/PDF strokes so it can receive different laser settings. It does not cut the 3D solid, and omitted tabs receive no marking.

Each panel uses separate upper and lower support planes based on the corresponding untwisted root and tip surfaces. Tab feet account for the actual twist and rib thickness. Finished outlines and solids are checked against each enabled plane; tabbed manufacturing contours use a 0.005 mm curve approximation. The planes are airfoil-based, not spar-based, and twisted rib bodies may remain clear of the tab-foot line.

All tab options are saved in projects and Edit > Defaults. Older projects retain their bottom-tab settings; top tabs and the sheeting override default to off.

Add Rib 1a
Panel 1 only. Adds a center-section reinforcement rib midway between the first two regular stations; later rib labels adjust accordingly.
Lightening Holes
Creates optimized circular rib holes. Start Rib and Stop Rib select the range; Min Border Distance retains material around rib edges and structural openings, and Min Hole Distance separates neighboring holes. Add this option last because cutting the holes can significantly increase generation time.
Riblets
Adds partial ribs over the selected Start Rib to End Rib range. Riblets per Bay selects 1–5 intermediate riblets in each bay. Riblets require a carbon-fiber leading edge and a mid-position CF spar between 20% and 40% chord; the option is unavailable without these parts.

Rib Caps tab

Top Rib Caps and Bottom Rib Caps independently add cap strips along the clear sections of each full rib. Riblets are excluded and receive neither caps nor cap recesses. Both start unchecked and are also available in Defaults.

Checking an option reveals Thickness (initially 1/16 inch, 1.5875 mm) and Width (initially 1/4 inch, 6.35 mm). Width runs across the rib; Thickness is recessed into its top or bottom surface so the finished cap follows the airfoil. These fields use the global units and accept explicit mm or in values, like other length fields.

Caps stop at spars, sheeting, spoilers, edge stock, controls, and other occupied areas. When a build tab overlaps a rib cap, generation completes with a construction warning identifying the panel and rib. The temporary tab passes through the cap; the cap remains unchanged in the preview and STEP assembly. Remove construction tabs before finishing the wing. Root and tip caps stay within the panel end faces; very thin nose and tail regions retain a small rib core. Caps appear in the wing preview, plan, and STEP assembly.

Cap collision checks run in parallel across ribs, using the configured thread limit. The status bar identifies the rib and cap side being checked, for example Checking rib cap collisions for Rib 11 (Top; ...).

Spars tab

Use Add Spar to create any number of spar definitions. Check spar headings and press Delete Checked to remove selected rows.

Root Chord Location
Position as a percentage of the root airfoil chord.
Tip Chord Location
Position as a percentage of the tip airfoil chord. Editable in Manual mode; calculated and disabled when a Parallel to reference is selected. The spar is extruded in a straight line between the root and tip positions.
Parallel to

The dropdown below Root/Tip Chord Location lists spanwise geometry in the current panel only. Its choices refresh when opened and include other spars, fixed/removable joiners, alignment pins, aileron/flap hinges and their spars or TE, LE/TE edges, sheeting boundaries, shear webs, turbulators, and spoilers where present. The spar itself is excluded.

Set Root Chord Location, then choose a reference such as Fixed Joiner 1 or Trailing Edge. The spar stays straight through its root position and follows the reference's direction in plan view. Curved references use their end-to-end direction; the spar does not follow the curve. Top / Bottom / Mid continues to determine height. The disabled Tip Chord Location displays the calculated percentage.

Manual (Tip Chord Location) restores the previously entered manual tip value and enables editing. Selected references are saved with projects and update when their geometry changes. Deleted references, circular dependencies, and directions that put the tip outside the allowed 0–90% chord range must be corrected before generation; change the root location, select another reference, or return to Manual.

Automatic rear-collision relocation switches the affected spar to Manual so it can follow the obstruction's front edge. Rejected moves restore its original reference and root/tip locations.

Places the spar against the upper surface, against the lower surface, or through the airfoil interior.
Material: Wood / CF
Wood reveals Height and Width for rectangular stock and matching rib notches. CF reveals the Type choices below.
CF Type: Tube
Creates a carbon tube using OD (outside diameter) and ID (inside diameter), with aligned rib holes. ID must be smaller than OD.
CF Type: Rod
Creates a solid carbon rod using OD.
CF Type: Strip
Creates rectangular carbon strip stock using Width and Thickness.
Shear Webs
Available when matching top and bottom wood spars share the same root and tip chord locations. Thickness sets the web sheet thickness. Creates individual web parts between rib faces.

Generate Wing alignment warning

Pressing Generate Wing checks spars and joiners in each panel. When a spar has the same root chord percentage as a joiner but a different plan-view direction, one warning dialog lists all affected pairs for that button press. Continue proceeds with generation without changing the settings; Cancel stops before generation so you can select the corresponding joiner in Parallel to. A later button press checks again. Equal percentages alone do not imply parallel directions on a tapered or swept wing. New-install defaults explicitly link both wood spars to the joiner.

Sheeting tab

Sheet Entire Panel enables Front Top, Front Bottom, Rear Top, and Rear Bottom Sheeting and sets all four Stop Rib Numbers to the panel's last rib. Press it again after changing the rib count to apply the new last rib.

Front Top Sheeting / Front Bottom Sheeting
Each checkbox reveals Thickness, Stop Rib Number, Stop Chord Location, and Up To Spar. Recesses the matching rib surface and builds curved sheeting from the leading-edge system. Front sheeting is resolved before rear sheeting and spar solids so adjoining sheets meet cleanly. When Front sheeting extends beyond a spar, DesignRC recesses the spar beneath the sheeting so the sheeting covers it while remaining on the airfoil surface.
Stop Chord Location
Ends Front sheeting at the entered percentage of chord. If the location intersects a spar, DesignRC moves the boundary to the far edge of that spar.
Up To Spar
Ends Front sheeting at the applicable spar instead of the Stop Chord Location. Clear this option to enter the stop location manually.
Rear Top Sheeting / Rear Bottom Sheeting
Each checkbox reveals Thickness and Stop Rib Number. Builds recessed curved sheeting aft from the spar region and butts it against TE sheeting where their ranges meet.
Stop Rib Number
Last rib covered by a sheet that begins at the panel root.
Turbulators
Count selects 1–4 longitudinal strips; Height and Width define their rectangular stock cross-section. Front sheeting is divided around enabled turbulators.

Joiners tab

Use Fixed Panel or Removable Panel to choose the panel joint type. Changing panel type deletes incompatible joiner rows after confirmation.

Fixed Panel

Add Fixed Joiner creates rows at an entered chord location. A fixed joiner may be:

  • a one-piece V-shaped wood joiner aligned with matching upper and lower wood spars;
  • a carbon-fiber tube or rod; or
  • a steel rod.

Each fixed row provides Chord Location, Number of Rib Bays, and material choices Wood, CF, or Steel Rod. Wood uses Thickness; CF offers Tube with OD/ID or Rod with OD; Steel Rod uses OD. Check the row heading and use Delete Checked to remove it.

Removable Panel

Add Sleeve/Rod Joiner and Add Alignment Pin add the corresponding rows. Chord Location sets their root chord percentage. Sleeve/rod joiners also provide Number of Rib Bays.

Sleeve/Rod Joiner
Defines which side of the joint carries the sleeve and rod, their materials, diameters, and corresponding adjoining-panel parts. Each sleeve has OD and ID fields; a new ID defaults to OD minus 1 mm. ID follows OD changes until you enter a different ID. Startup sleeve dimensions match on both sides; Edit > Defaults can specify independent sizes. Alignment sleeves also have ID fields. Sleeve IDs are saved in projects; older projects retain their previously inferred bores. The rod remains straight through the joint and extends into the sleeve so the panels can slide together for flight and apart for transport.
Alignment Pin
Defines either a sleeve/pin arrangement or a pin/hole arrangement, including side, material, and diameter.
This Panel / Adjoining Panel
Choose Sleeve or Rod on each side of a sleeve/rod joint. Sleeve materials are CF, Aluminum, Steel, or Fiberglass, with OD and ID; rod materials are CF or Steel, with OD. Alignment pins offer Sleeve/Pin or Pin/Hole, the applicable side selection, and sleeve or pin dimensions and materials.
Delete Checked
Removes the selected fixed or removable rows.

Joiner axes and rib openings are oriented through the joint geometry so panels assemble at the intended dihedral. Fixed and removable tube or rod joiners are raised above the camber line at each joint rib by the same amount that they fall below the camber line at the adjoining rib, preserving equal rib material above and below the joiner. Alignment pins are excluded from this balancing rule. Each fixed joiner and removable sleeve/rod joiner has a Number of Rib Bays field. The integer count starts at 1 and cannot exceed the physical rib bays in the panel. One bay passes through two ribs, two bays through three ribs, and so on; optional R1a counts as a physical rib. The same count applies on both sides of a joint and must fit the adjoining panel too. Circular joiners, sleeves, rods, and wood joiners reach the far face of the selected terminal rib. Every traversed wood-joiner rib, including the terminal rib, is bisected into two manufacturing pieces; intersecting shear webs are omitted over the selected span. Alignment pins retain their existing lengths and have no bay-count field. Generate Wing checks that the full joiner section stays within each traversed rib at its center plane and both faces. A joiner leaving a rib reports "Joiner N exits rib RN. Decrease dihedral or joiner length." These settings are saved in projects and Edit > Defaults. Invalid wood-spar alignment is rejected because a wood joiner cannot bend through a swept joint. Joiners are also checked against other joiners, including overlapping wood joiners.

LE/TE tab

Imported LE/TE curves

Use Import LE/TE Curves to define this panel with exactly two open paths in an SVG or ASCII DXF file. The filename appears below the button. DesignRC uses X for chord and positive Y for span. If the combined bounds of the two paths are longer along X than Y, DesignRC automatically swaps their X and Y coordinates before checking the roots and span. Equal extents leave the axes unchanged. DXF curves must have exactly Z=0. The SVG origin is its user-coordinate origin after element transforms, independent of viewBox canvas bounds. SVG width and height must declare mm or inches; DXF must declare mm or inches in $INSUNITS. Pixel-only and unitless files are rejected.

After any axis swap, exactly one root endpoint must be within 1/4 inch (6.35 mm) of the origin to identify the LE. Both roots must be within that distance of the X axis (Y=0). Each path's Y span must be within 1/4 inch (6.35 mm) of Panel Span. DesignRC translates both paths by the same X amount and independently normalizes their Y ranges to 0 through Panel Span. Chords are preserved. Paths that cross, loop, double back in Y, or give zero chord are rejected.

Root Chord and Tip Chord become read-only values from the curves. Tip Sweep is disabled and displays -- because the imported LE defines sweep. Delete LE/TE Curves returns to a straight planform using the derived chords and sweep, with the usual inherited root chord on outer panels. The curve geometry and filename, including any panel-joint correction, are embedded in saved projects. Reopening restores the geometry and the filename below the import button without rereading or needing the original file. To use later edits to the source file, import it again. Import and deletion use the usual pending-generation behavior.

SVG supports open paths (lines, cubic/quadratic Beziers and elliptical arcs), lines, and polylines, including group transforms. DXF supports LINE, ARC, ELLIPSE, open POLYLINE/LWPOLYLINE (including bulges), and control-point SPLINE entities. Standalone POINT markers are ignored and do not count as paths. Flatten nested SVG viewports, CSS transforms, DXF blocks, and unsupported entities before importing.

LE stock follows the imported curve for every stock type. Selecting CF Tube LE with an imported curve displays a warning: the user must determine whether the chosen tube can bend to the modeled shape. Spars remain straight between their specified endpoints. Clearance is checked between ribs as well as at ribs.

At panel joints, an ordinary outer panel inherits the inner panel's tip chord. An outer panel with imported curves must have a root chord within 1/4 in (6.35 mm) of that tip chord. DesignRC then matches the outer root by shifting its TE at the root and reducing that correction linearly to zero at the tip, preserving the LE and tip chord. Larger differences, or corrections that make the curves cross, stop generation with an error. This also applies when the inner panel uses imported curves; source files are unchanged.

Imported stock follows a smooth 3D guide sweep. Wood stock, sheeting, and control surfaces retain their changing airfoil profiles and panel twist; CF rods and tubes use circular sweeps. Straight geometry uses a single root-to-tip loft when that reproduces the intermediate profiles. Construction gaps still create separate pieces. If OCCT cannot form a valid sweep, DesignRC tries a smooth loft with a limited number of profiles; overly complex geometry is rejected instead of building hundreds of loft sections.

Leading edge

Block LE Stock
Removes the rib noses at the selected width and lofts smooth wood stock from the airfoil profiles at the member ends. Height must accommodate every cut rib; equality at the displayed precision is allowed; after an undersized-height warning is acknowledged, the Height control is reset to the required cut-face height.
Molded LE Sheet
Curved sheeting wraps around the leading-edge nose following the interpolated airfoil along the span. Width is the total surface distance around the nose, split equally between upper and lower surfaces, and defaults to 5/8 inch (15.875 mm). Thickness defaults to 1/16 inch (1.5875 mm); the rib is recessed inward for the sheet. Increase Width or reduce Thickness if the wrap is too narrow to leave a usable rib core. The wrap is treated as sheeting for rib-cap clearance and the build-tab sheeting option.
Notched LE
A stick fits a centered horizontal notch in the rib nose. Width, defaulting to 3/8 inch (9.525 mm), runs aft from the nose to the back of the notch and includes the material rounded to the airfoil at the front. Height is the notch's vertical opening, centered on the nose, and defaults to 3/16 inch (4.7625 mm). If the stock cannot meet both rib surfaces, increase Width or reduce Height. The exposed nose follows the interpolated airfoil along the span.
Diamond LE (Square Balsa Rotated 45 Degrees)
Selects square balsa stock rotated 45 degrees and reveals a Width field. Width is the square's side length, defaults to 1/4 inch (6.35 mm), follows global units, and accepts explicit inch or millimeter entry. The stock's forward corner sits at the original airfoil leading edge; its two aft faces cut a straight V into each rib. The airfoil intersects those faces, so the exposed notch edges may be shorter than the stock's full side length. If Width is too small for both aft faces to meet the rib surfaces, generation increases it to the size needed by all ribs in the panel and updates the Width field. The exposed leading edge is rounded to the interpolated airfoil shape along the span; the two straight V faces remain intact. The stock is included in the preview and STEP assembly, and the V appears in exported rib outlines. Diamond LE is mutually exclusive with Block LE Stock, CF Tube LE, and CF Rod LE. Its selection and Width are available in Edit > Defaults and saved with projects.
CF Tube LE
Creates an exposed carbon tube and circular rib cradles. OD defines the outside; ID defines the bore.
CF Rod LE
Creates a solid exposed carbon rod and matching rib cradles.

Molded LE Sheet and Notched LE are centered vertically on the airfoil nose, with material extending aft into the rib. They belong to the exclusive LE radio-button group. Their fields follow global units and accept explicit inch or millimeter input, including fractions. All options are saved in projects and available in Edit > Defaults. Rib outlines include their recesses, and the wood parts appear in the preview and STEP assembly.

Trailing edge

Sheet TE Stock
Removes the rear rib profile and lofts smooth sheet stock matching the airfoil-derived top and bottom surfaces. It cannot be combined with Top or Bottom TE Sheeting. An undersized Height is reset to the required cut-face height after the warning is acknowledged.
Slotted for Ribs
Extends ribs into open-ended slots in the trailing-edge stock for keyed assembly.
Top / Bottom TE Sheeting
Builds independent trailing-edge sheets of constant panel-wide Width and the selected Thickness. These options are mutually exclusive with Sheet TE Stock; top and bottom TE sheets may be used together.
Taper Sheeting
Tapers the TE sheet from its Taper Start Location to the trailing edge. Taper Start Location is a percentage of the sheet width measured aft from its front edge. When both TE sheets are enabled, both taper options are required automatically.

Ailerons/Flaps tab

Perpendicular to Ribs, above Width and Height for each control, makes the hinge spanwise and perpendicular to the rib chord direction. Width becomes Root Width, measured at the control's first rib; the tip width follows the wing's taper, sweep, or imported TE. This option is saved in projects and available in Edit > Defaults. It is separate from the imported-curve Hinge Parallel with Y Axis option, which measures maximum width. The two options are mutually exclusive. A hinge that crosses the LE or TE is rejected.

Ailerons and flaps can be solid controls or built-up assemblies of ribs, stock, spars, and sheeting. Solid controls use separate closed solids with airfoil-derived start and end profiles. Imported planforms add intermediate geometry sections to follow the curved trailing edge without adding physical ribs.

With imported curves, each enabled control has Hinge Parallel with Y Axis below Width/Height. Unchecked, Width sets the chordwise distance from the TE at each end and a straight line connects those hinge endpoints; the interior may be wider. Checked, the hinge is parallel to Y and Width is the maximum chordwise width. A hinge that crosses the TE or leaves insufficient room for its post is rejected.

Width
Chordwise depth measured forward from the trailing edge.
Height
Nominal control body height setting.
Start / Stop Rib
Spanwise boundaries. Flaps stop before a panel tip; an aileron may extend through the tip rib.
Hinge Post Width / Height
Cross-section of the separate fixed hinge-post solid ahead of the movable control.
Wiring Holes
Adds rectangular wiring openings through the selected ribs using start/end ribs, chord location, width, and height.
  • A clearance gap is maintained around movable controls.
  • For straight planforms, meeting flap and aileron widths must match and share a continuous hinge post. For imported curves, their hinge endpoints must meet; each straight post follows its own hinge direction.
  • Aileron Start Rib cannot precede Flap Stop Rib.

Built-up ailerons and flaps

Select Built-up Aileron or Built-up Flap to replace the solid movable surface with separate ribs. Choose one hinge construction: Hinge Post reveals Hinge Post Width and Height and creates matching wing-side and movable-side posts; Top/Bottom Spar reveals Height, Width, and Hinge Angle and creates top and bottom wood spars on both sides of the hinge gap. New hinge-spar defaults are 1/4 inch (6.35 mm) Height, 3/8 inch (9.525 mm) Width, and 35 degrees Hinge Angle. Hinge Angle is the aft bevel from vertical: the top edge of the control is closest to the wing and the bottom is farther aft. The moving rib and spars are cut to the same bevel. Open spar notches leave stock at the gap for sanding to the airfoil surface; the spar faces are trimmed flush with the bevel. All four spars end flush with the outside faces of the movable end ribs. Invalid dimensions or an angle that leaves no usable rib produce the usual geometry warning. These settings also appear in Edit > Defaults.

Independent Top Sheeting and Bottom Sheeting provide Thickness and use the same construction and rib recesses as wing TE sheeting. Each selected sheet automatically extends from the aft face of its hinge post or spar to the TE; there is no skin Width parameter. Existing wing TE sheeting or stock is retained, and the control skin butts against it. Built-up control sheeting has no manual taper controls. When both sides meet at the TE, their thicknesses are resolved automatically to fit. These options do not change the wing's TE stock or sheeting settings. Built-up controls retain the selected wing TE stock; their ribs end at that stock, with sheeting butted against it. A warning reports when the hinge construction and TE stock leave no room for a rib.

Add Spar and Delete Spar manage checked spar rows. Root/Tip Chord Location is measured from the movable surface's front edge (0%) to its TE (100%). Wood and CF tube/rod/strip options match the Spars tab; vertical location is Top or Bottom. Surface spars remain straight; their axes are recessed as needed to clear changing airfoils, twist, and selected sheeting throughout the control span. Stock too large to fit is rejected. Built-up ribs appear in parts exports, and the separate construction is included in plans and STEP assemblies. Control TE stock uses ATE1, ATE2, etc. for ailerons and FTE1, FTE2, etc. for flaps, numbered separately across panels. These names appear on exported parts and in STEP assemblies; plan leaders show each stock part name, Width, and Height. All options are saved with projects and User Defaults.

Hinge Post Height must be at least the adjoining wing-side and movable-side rib cut heights. An undersized value reports the required minimum and updates the field; generate again after the correction. Equality is accepted. Movable ribs use ARn (aileron) and FRn (flap) throughout plans, rib drawings, the Export Parts dialog, exported part labels and filenames, and STEP assemblies. Plans separate labels at shared flap/aileron joints and include leaders for each control spar and skin thickness. Wing TE sheeting continues into built-up controls and recesses their exported ribs. The fixed-wing and movable sheets stop on their respective sides of hinge and spanwise end gaps, including shared flap/aileron joints. Additional control skins butt against that TE sheet without adding a second overlapping layer.

Spoilers tab

Spoilers
Builds a recessed spoiler panel, forward and aft frame rails, and end support rails over the selected rib range.
Start / End Rib
Defines the spoiler span. A center spoiler requires zero dihedral.
Start Chord Location
Positions the spoiler assembly as a percentage of local chord when Immediately Behind Spar is unchecked. The spoiler may sit between front and rear spars; actual overlaps are reported during generation.

Shown below Start Chord Location when a top spar exists. Checking it disables Start Chord Location and places the spoiler forward frame rail directly behind the spar. With no top spar, the option is hidden and manual chord placement stays available.

Chordwise depth of the moving spoiler panel. The complete assembly also needs room for both frame rails and the clearance gaps.
Spoiler Thickness
Thickness of the moving panel and frame rails.

Width of the surrounding frame rails. Support Rail Height is calculated and displayed.

Adds optimized holes to the spoiler panel. Min Border Distance retains material around its outline, and Min Circle Distance separates neighboring holes.
Wiring Holes
The same synchronized wiring-hole definition shown on the Ailerons/Flaps page.

Spoiler panels, frame rails, and support rails are checked against spars, the trailing edge, and the other spoiler parts. Collision messages identify both conflicting components.

Generate Wing

Generate Wing validates every panel and then builds ribs, openings, members, controls, joiners, material solids, the mirrored assembly, and display meshes. Progress messages identify the active stage. Cancel interrupts rib lightening-hole layout, hole cutting, and rib meshing, including parallel workers. Other individual solid-modeling operations may need to finish before cancellation takes effect.

Successful generation enables Generate Plan, Export Parts, and Export STEP. A failed generation reports the specific panel or component and leaves the last valid preview intact.

Built-up controls report separate aileron and flap progress, including rib counts, individual stock, sheeting, spars, clearance checks, and display meshing. Panel completion is reported only after its controls are finished. The overall percentage includes control construction, and multi-panel status identifies a panel still working.

Plan View and plan exports

Generate Plan creates a flattened drawing of both wing halves at manufacturing dimensions. It includes rib outlines and labels, spars, sheeting, edge stock, controls, spoilers, hinge posts or hinge spars, joiners, dimensions, panel spans, dihedral and twist notes, metrics, borders, and a title block. Sheeting leaders are routed to open areas between ribs and spars so labels do not obscure parts.

Left drag
Pans the drawing.
Mouse wheel
Zooms the drawing.
Fit View
Fits the full plan when Plan View is active.
Export Plan
Choose PDF, DXF, or SVG and click OK, then choose a destination and filename. PDF uses a custom page sized to the complete drawing. DXF includes all outlines, leaders, borders, and outlined labels as CAD geometry in millimeters, preserving line and label colors using AutoCAD 2004 RGB colors. SVG retains line colors, fill colors, and transparency, with outlined labels and physical dimensions in millimeters. All three formats preserve the complete plan layout at 1:1 scale.
Changing any design parameter invalidates the plan. Generate Wing and Generate Plan again before exporting a revised plan.

Plan DXF and SVG contain geometry for outlines, leaders, borders, and labels. DXF stores line colors; SVG stores line and fill colors plus transparency. An importing CAD program may display its own sketch-status colors instead of the saved colors; Fusion sketches behave this way. Changing a file to DWG does not change that sketch display behavior. DesignRC does not export DWG.

Parts export

Export Parts lists all available ribs and split rib halves, riblets, shear webs, sheet-stock parts, wood joiners, spoilers, and one dihedral-angle template per panel. A rib bisected by a wood joiner is exported as two separate closed pieces in its part drawing.

DXF / SVG / PDF
Select one or more output formats. DXF and SVG are suitable for CAD and laser workflows. A combined PDF places all selected parts tightly on one custom-size poster page at full scale; trailing-edge stock is oriented with its length horizontal.
Initial selection
No parts are selected when the dialog opens. OK remains disabled until at least one format and one part are selected.

Exports all generated part drawings into one combined file for each selected format. The PDF is one poster-size page rather than letter-size tiles.

Checks or clears the entire list.

Creates a separate file named from each checked part.

DXF and SVG use millimeters for reliable CAD import. Exported ribs include generated holes, notches, slots, lightening geometry, and split-piece outlines. Curved outlines are exported without the occasional large corner loops produced by earlier spline handling.

STEP assembly export

Export STEP writes the current generated 3D model as a named assembly. Individual wood, carbon-fiber, aluminum, steel, and fiberglass components remain separate assembly parts with material colors. STEP export requires a current successful Generate Wing operation.

User Defaults

Edit > Defaults controls the starting state for new projects:

  • Global length unit: Millimetres (mm) or Inches (in);
  • Number of Wing Panels: the default number of connected panels;
  • Number of Threads: geometry worker threads, up to the available CPU thread count minus one;
  • complete parameter defaults for every panel, including independent Front/Rear Build Tabs, their Locations, shared Tab Width, Height Above Work Table, Tab/Rib Separation Line, and Top/Bottom Rib Caps (checkboxes start unchecked);
  • built-up aileron/flap skins and spars, Hinge Post/Top-Bottom Spar construction with Hinge Angle, and fixed/removable joiner Number of Rib Bays; and
  • per-parameter Global/mm/in choices and default rows for spars and joiners.

New projects use all saved settings. Saving Defaults also updates current project length fields that use different units from the selected global unit, as described below. A fresh installation uses the installed defaults, with build tabs and both rib-cap options unchecked.

Changing the global length unit in Defaults rounds lengths that use Global units: inches to whole millimeters (except 1/16 inch becomes 1.5 mm), or millimeters to the nearest 1/32 inch. Explicit mm/in overrides retain their values in Defaults. On Save, current project length fields displayed in other units receive the saved default dimensions and follow the new global unit; fields already displayed in the new unit retain their values. Regenerate the wing after these changes. New projects use all saved defaults. Cancel discards the default changes.

Validation and automatic corrections

DesignRC checks parameter ranges and geometric feasibility before and during generation. Examples include overlapping controls, shared control widths, spoiler-to-spar and spoiler-to-TE collisions, wiring-hole fit, riblet prerequisites, LE/TE stock height, spar and joiner collisions, joiner-to-joiner collisions, tube dimensions, wood-joiner alignment, and failed solid Boolean operations.

When only one valid correction exists, DesignRC may adjust a related field and explain the change. Otherwise generation stops with a message identifying what must be corrected.

A spar placed into TE stock, an aileron, or a flap reports a collision. Closing the message proposes Root and Tip Chord Locations that leave 0.25 mm clearance and run parallel to the obstruction's straight front edge where possible. Curved edges use a forward offset. Increasing the TE or control Width also checks the rear spar automatically. Moves that intersect other spars or the front stock are rejected. If full geometry generation finds another collision, the proposed move is rolled back to the original root and tip locations. Generate Wing again after a correction; a spar that cannot fit requires a design change.

An LE or hinge-post Height equal to the adjoining rib cut is valid. In Top/Bottom Spar hinge mode, the spar Width must exceed Height multiplied by the tangent of Hinge Angle; the pair must leave rib material between them, clear TE stock, and avoid other control spars. Moving rib bevels and exported outlines share the same geometry. After changing construction or sheeting, regenerate the wing before re-exporting previously saved parts.

Manufacturing responsibility: inspect exported geometry, material choices, clearances, structural loads, and machine settings before cutting or flying. DesignRC does not certify structural strength or airworthiness.
